# Environment migration: from generated scenery to free, high-quality assets

Written for 1.2.0 (November 2026). It covers an audit of the environment as built, the free assets worth using, the
migration plan and its status, step-by-step instructions, and an honest before/after assessment. Licence and listing
facts were checked by web search in November 2026. Unity's own pages could not be opened from the build machine, so
**check each listing before importing.**

---

## 1. Audit: what the reserve is made of today

The whole environment is **generated by the editor builder** (`WildLens ▸ Vertical Slice Builder`) into one root
object on top of the open scene's Terrain. Nothing is hand-placed, and the builder is the only way content gets into
the scene. Any migration has to work *through* the builder, or the next Rebuild discards it.

| Area | How it is made today | Source files | Quality (honest) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Terrain shape** | The user's terrain if the scene has one; otherwise a generated 900 m fallback (two octaves of Perlin noise plus a rim). The pond basin, overlook hill, creek channel and road grade are carved into it (backed up first). | `WildLensSliceBuilder.GenerateFallbackHeights`, `ReserveBuilder`, `EcosystemBuilder`, `RoadBuilder` | Gentle and readable, but noise-shaped: no drainage logic, no real ridgelines, weak vistas. The reserve's look depends on the terrain it's built on. |
| **Terrain surface** | Five layers painted by ecology (grass, meadow, forest floor, dirt, rock) from slope, forest density, tree proximity, trail, creek and road distance. Scanned CC0 textures (Poly Haven) with normal maps; generated textures as fallback. | `TerrainPainter.cs` | Good painting logic. Weak source material: **no CC0 scan of lush grass exists**, so both grass layers are a tinted leaf-litter scan. Tiling shows on open ground at distance (no anti-tiling shader). |
| **Grass and flowers** | Unity terrain details: six billboard prototypes (short grass, meadow grass, yellow/white/purple flowers, fern) whose textures are **drawn procedurally in code**. | `TerrainPainter.PaintDetails`, `DrawGrass` … | Density and placement are good. The blades are flat, generated sprites: the most "prototype" thing on screen at 4–16× zoom. |
| **Trees** | Procedural meshes: whorled conifers, pine form, three broadleaf forms (spreading, ovate, multi-stem birch) with limbs and CC0 leaf/needle cards. ~420 scaled to the reserve, plus a 72-tree grove and three giants (the Grandmother Oak). Distance culled with one-level LODGroups. | `VegetationBuilder` (tree pass), `FoliageCards`, `ReserveBuilder.BuildGrove` | The single largest environment weakness: cards lit like paper (no translucency), no real LODs or billboards, few silhouettes. Reads as "generated" in photos. |
| **Shrubs and undergrowth** | CC0 scanned shrubs (Poly Haven Shrub 02/04) within 30 m of the trail, lobed placeholder bushes elsewhere; ~160 CC0 ferns under woodland. | `VegetationBuilder.BuildBushes/BuildUndergrowth` | Scans are good up close. Placeholder bushes beyond 30 m mix fidelities. |
| **Rocks** | CC0 scanned mossy rock pieces in outcrop clusters, one scanned boulder for landmarks, procedural blobs for the rest. **1.2:** two scanned hillside rock faces on steep slopes and the ridge. | `VegetationBuilder.BuildRocks/BuildOutcrops`, `ArtLibrary` | Good where scanned. Before 1.2 the ridge had no exposed bedrock. |
| **Logs, stumps, deadwood** | CC0 scanned fallen trunk and stump; procedural cylinders as fallback; procedural snags. | `BuildLogs`, `ReserveBuilder` | Good where scanned; snags are procedural. |
| **Water** | Custom flat water surfaces for the pond and Willow Creek, realtime reflection probe at the pond, procedural reeds and cattails. | `EcosystemBuilder`, `ReserveBuilder.BuildPond` | Static (no flow or ripples). |
| **Props and landmarks** | Primitive boxes and cylinders with flat-coloured materials: kiosk, signs (TextMesh), decks, blind, bridges, benches, telescope, cairn, canoe, bird feeder. | `PropBuilder`, `ReserveBuilder`, `EcosystemBuilder` | Functional and well placed; flat-coloured boxes beside photographic ground. Landmarks *as gameplay* (destinations, residents, arrival notes, best times) are strong and must be kept. |
| **Lighting** | One directional sun aimed at the sun in the HDRI; sky-based ambient and reflections; exponential fog matched to the horizon colour; shadows 160 m, 4 cascades. URP grading volume (ACES, white balance, bloom, vignette). | `WildLensSliceBuilder.ConfigureLighting`, `BuildPostProcessing` | Good. No baked or probe GI, so shade under the grove's canopy is flat. |
| **Sky and time of day** | Two CC0 HDRIs (late morning, golden hour) swapped and rotated through dawn to dusk; sun, fog and grading keyed by hour; dawn mist patches. | `DayCycle`, `MistPatch` | Good mood range. No night sky, no overcast. |
| **Weather** | None. | – | Missing (planned as the next "rhythm"). |
| **Wind** | A gust field drives procedural sway on generated plants; a WindZone drives prefab/SpeedTree trees and terrain grass. | `WildLensWind`, `FoliageSway`, `BuildWind` | Good. |

**Gameplay contracts the environment must keep** (anything that replaces scenery must honour them):

* Trees carry an `EnvironmentFeature` (Tree, trunk radius, height): squirrels climb, birds perch, owls roost.
  Rocks, logs and bushes carry theirs too (perches, cover).
* Trees have a **solid trunk collider** (the player can't walk through) and a **trigger crown collider**, which blocks
  photo line of sight without blocking movement. Shrubs are triggers: they hide a crouched player and block photos.
* Placement respects the path and road clearances, creek exclusion, keep-outs (decks, blind, ring around the
  Grandmother Oak), view cones and density zones. Destinations' residents, hotspots and schedule anchors are
  unaffected by what the scenery looks like.
* Everything is generated: improvements land by rebuilding, never by hand edits.

## 2. Constraints that shape the migration

1. **This is a public git repository, and only CC0 art may be committed.** Asset Store packages, Unity's free ones
   included, are under the Asset Store EULA (or the Unity Companion License), which allows use in your game but not
   redistribution. So **no store asset can be committed**. They must be imported by each user into their own
   project, and WildLens has to *find and use* them.
2. **The scene is rebuilt from code.** "Reuse a whole demo scene" can't mean opening it and playing. It means either
   building the reserve on the demo's terrain, or harvesting the demo's trees, grass and terrain layers into the
   builder. 1.2 does the second (see §4, stage 6, for why not the first, yet).
3. **URP is the primary target, with Built-in as a fallback; HDRP is not supported.** That rules out the best-known
   free Unity environments (Book of the Dead, Fontainebleau), which are HDRP-only.
4. **No mixing of fidelities** (AGENTS.md §9). Stylised or low-poly packs beside photographic ground look worse than
   either alone. Free stylised packs are rejected even when good.
5. **Package size.** `Art~` ships to every user (65 MB before 1.2), so CC0 additions must earn their megabytes.

## 4. Migration plan and status

The plan keeps a playable build at every step: each stage is optional, reversible by rebuilding, and changes
nothing for a player who doesn't opt in.

| Stage | What | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Fix the safety net.** The committed compile check couldn't run: its `Check.csproj` had never been committed (the repo-wide `.gitignore` excludes `*.csproj`). | **Done (1.2).** All five configurations compile; deliberate errors fail it. |
| 1 | **CC0 bedrock**: scanned rock faces on slopes and the ridge. Visible after Update + Rebuild with no imports. | **Done (1.2).** Needs a screenshot check. |
| 2 | **Environment Kit**: free packs sorted by ecological role and placed by the builder's existing rules, with gameplay components added automatically; the project scanner. | **Done (1.2).** Compiled and unit-checked outside Unity; **not yet run in Unity**. |
| 3 | **User imports Tier 1 packs, scans, rebuilds, sends screenshots** (steps in §5). | **Next: needs the user.** |
| 4 | **Tune from screenshots**: role assignments, `treeScale`, grass density per slot, which layers fill which slots. Fix whatever the first real run exposes. | After stage 3. |
| 5 | **Atmosphere**: an overcast sky and weather as a second rhythm (CC0 HDRIs); translucent-leaf look comes free with SpeedTree trees. Optionally Adaptive Probe Volumes for grove shade (needs a bake: conflicts with "rebuild, don't hand-edit"). | Planned. |
| 6 | **Build on the demo's terrain** (the "reuse the whole scene" option): open the demo scene and build the reserve on it. | **Not supported yet.** The builder expects one Terrain, while Unity's demo is split into tiles. The demo's own tree instances would stand on the new paths, and the painter would repaint its surface. It needs multi-terrain support, clearing tree instances in clearances, and a "keep host surface" option. Worth doing only if stage 3's screenshots show the harvested assets aren't enough. |
| 7 | A **hand-authored reserve terrain** (Terrain Tools) shipped as CC0 heightmap data, so vistas no longer depend on noise. | Planned (AGENTS.md §8, item 8). |

## 5. How to use it (for the playtester)

1. **Update the package** (Package Manager ▸ WildLens ▸ Update), then **Rebuild** the slice. You get the new rock
   outcrops with no imports.
2. Use **Unity 6000.0.37 or newer** (older 6000.0 versions don't render the demo's trees).
3. In Package Manager ▸ **My Assets**, add and import **Unity Terrain – URP Demo Scene**, then **Terrain Sample Asset
   Pack**. Accept any packages they ask for (Terrain Tools, Shader Graph). Import into your own project; nothing goes
   into the WildLens package.
4. **WildLens ▸ Vertical Slice Builder ▸ Scan Project for Nature Assets.** A dialog lists what was found. The kit
   (`Assets/WildLens/Data/EnvironmentKit.asset`) is selected: look through each role and drag out anything that is
   in the wrong place. The report at the bottom lists what was rejected and why.
5. **Rebuild Slice In Open Scene**, press Play.
6. Send screenshots of: the trail start, the grove, the creek near a bridge, a meadow at 4× zoom (grass) and the
   ridge, ideally from **WildLens ▸ Capture Comparison Shots** before *and* after the scan.

To go back: clear the **Environment Kit** field in the builder settings and Rebuild. The reserve returns to the bundled art exactly as before.

**Settings worth knowing:** `treeScale` (packs model trees at different sizes); `replaceAllGeneratedTrees` (on: never
mix generated and kit trees); `mixWithBundledArt` (on: kit rocks, shrubs and ferns join the CC0 scans; off: they
replace them); each grass slot's `density` (mesh grass covers more than a billboard; start at 0.5).

## 6. Before/after assessment

No Unity run was possible while writing this, so the "after" column is **expected** and needs screenshots to confirm.

| Area | Before (1.1) | After 1.2, no imports | After 1.2 + Tier 1 packs (expected) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trees | Procedural, paper-lit cards, no LODs | Unchanged | SpeedTree trees with LODs, wind and proper foliage shading, placed by role (conifers, creek-side, grove giants, edge saplings) | Medium-high: depends on the demo's species fitting a temperate reserve |
| Grass | Code-drawn billboards | Unchanged | Instanced mesh grass and plants from the sample pack | Medium: density and colour need tuning |
| Terrain surface | Tinted leaf-litter scan as grass | Unchanged | Demo/sample layers where named slots match | Medium: depends on how the layers are named |
| Rocks | Scanned pieces and boulder; no bedrock | **Scanned outcrops on steep slopes and the ridge** | Plus any kit rocks | Medium: orientation logic is measured but unplayed |
| Shrubs | Scans near the trail, placeholder lobes beyond | Unchanged | Kit shrubs everywhere, no placeholder lobes | Medium |
| Lighting, sky, atmosphere | HDRI day cycle, fog, grading | Unchanged | Unchanged (stage 5) | – |
| Gameplay | – | Unchanged (empty kit: identical rebuild) | Unchanged by design (colliders, features, clearances) | High for placement, medium for photo occlusion on kit trees (the crown trigger is approximate) |

**How to judge it.** Compare captures from the same viewpoints and ask the questions this project has always asked:
does anything read as generated at 4× and 16× zoom; do the woods have depth (trunks, crowns, shade) rather than
repetition; does the ground read as grass rather than litter; do fidelities match side by side? Success is the user
saying the reserve feels like a real place to walk through, not a prototype.

## 7. What to watch for in the first real run

* **Pink objects**: a shader for the wrong pipeline. The scanner rejects these, but materials created later can still
  break; run Window ▸ Rendering ▸ Render Pipeline Converter.
* **Trees too big or small**: set `treeScale`. Giants are scaled ×1.1–1.3 on top of that.
* **Frame rate**: SpeedTree trees with LODs should be *cheaper* than the generated trees (which have no LODs). If not,
  enable the GPU Resident Drawer (see `VisualRoadmap.md`, Unity 6 notes).
* **Photo blocking**: kit trees get a generic crown trigger if the prefab has none. If animals behind thin trees can't
  be photographed, or animals behind dense crowns can, that's the place to tune (`KitPlacement.EnsureTreeGameplay`).
* **Squirrels and perching birds** use each tree's `EnvironmentFeature` height: check they climb kit trees.
